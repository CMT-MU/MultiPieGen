Definitions#

This page defines the quantities provided by MultiPieGen. How to get the actual function forms and \(U\) matrices from the data is described in a later section. The harmonics and the SAMB are labeled by the multipole key PGMultipoleType and written with its LaTeX symbol, e.g. \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(T_{1u})\); both are explained in the last section, Multipole key. A Japanese version is here.

1. Point-group harmonics#

Spherical harmonics. The harmonics of rank l are built from

\[ O_{lm}(\boldsymbol{r}) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2l+1}}\, r^l\, Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi), \qquad m = l, l-1, \dots, -l, \]

homogeneous polynomials of degree l (e.g. \(O_{10} = z\), \(O_{1,\pm1} = \mp(x \pm iy)/\sqrt{2}\)). Their real combinations are the tesseral harmonics (\(m > 0\))

\[ C_{lm} = \frac{(-1)^m O_{lm} + O_{l,-m}}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad S_{lm} = \frac{(-1)^m O_{lm} - O_{l,-m}}{\sqrt{2}\, i}, \qquad C_{l0} = O_{l0}, \]

e.g. \(C_{11} = x\), \(S_{11} = y\), \(C_{22} = \sqrt{3}(x^2 - y^2)/2\), \(S_{22} = \sqrt{3}xy\).

Point-group harmonics. A point-group harmonics (X, l, Gamma, n) of a point group is a set of rank-l functions that forms a basis of the irrep Gamma. Its component gamma is

\[ X_{l\Gamma n}^{(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{r}) = \sum_{m} U_{m\gamma}\, O_{lm}(\boldsymbol{r}), \]

with \(U\) ((2l+1) x dim, rows m = l, ..., -l), whose columns are orthonormal.

Example (Oh). The dipole \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(T_{1u}) = (x, y, z)\) has

\[\begin{split} U = \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \end{pmatrix} \quad (m = 1, 0, -1), \end{split}\]

e.g. \(x = (-O_{11} + O_{1,-1})/\sqrt{2}\), and the quadrupole \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(E_{g}) = \left(z^2 - \frac{x^2+y^2}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 - y^2)\right)\).

  • Top groups. The harmonics are defined once for the two top groups, Oh (cubic) and D6h (trigonal and hexagonal), up to rank 11. Every one of the 47 point groups (32 crystallographic, 5 other settings -1, 10 complex -c) takes its harmonics from them: each component is a component of a top harmonics times a sign, \(X_{\rm PG} = c\, X_{\rm top}\) with \(c = \pm 1\).

  • The representation matrices of the irreps follow from \(U\), and all harmonics of an irrep give the same matrices.

  • The irreps follow the order of the character table. Complex irreps (in the -c groups) end in a/b and have a single component.

  • Multiplicity. When an irrep appears more than once at the same rank, n = 1, 2, ... distinguishes the harmonics, e.g. C4h: \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(B_{g},1) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 - y^2)\) and \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(B_{g},2) = \sqrt{3}xy\).

  • Complex group. E.g. C4-c: \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(E^{(a)}) = (x + iy)/\sqrt{2}\) and \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(E^{(b)}) = (x - iy)/\sqrt{2}\).

  • Key: PGMultipoleType(X, l, Gamma, n, -1, 0, 0, "q") (see Multipole key).

Polar (Q) and axial (G) harmonics. An axial harmonics transforms with an extra factor det R under an improper operation R (a mirror, the inversion, or a rotoinversion). Both come from the same top harmonics, but in a point group a polar and an axial harmonics of the same function can belong to different irreps, and in the same irrep their components can be in another order or have other signs (the coefficient c and the component assigned differ between Q and G). So, for the same (l, Gamma, n), the functions of Q and G differ in general:

  • In the top groups Oh and D6h, the axial harmonics is the polar one with g and u interchanged in the irrep: the same function, components, and order (\(c = 1\)). E.g. Oh: \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(T_{1u}) = \mathbb{G}_{1}(T_{1g}) = (x, y, z)\).

  • In a group of proper rotations only (C1, C2, D2, C4, D4, C3, D3, D3-1, C6, D6, T, O), there is no improper operation, and Q and G are identical. E.g. C4: \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(E) = \mathbb{G}_{1}(E) = (x, y)\).

  • In the other groups, they differ. Examples:

2. PG multipolar harmonics#

A PG multipolar harmonics combines a point-group harmonics with an internal degree of freedom.

Internal degree of freedom. First, the internal degree of freedom is fixed by its rank s (s = 1: a vector; s = 2, 3: higher-rank tensors) and its type x: polar (x = q, e.g. a displacement or an electric polarization) or axial (x = g, e.g. a spin or a rotation). Its basis \(e_\nu\) is the set of rank-s orbitals of that type, of the cubic family (Oh) or of the hexagonal family (D6h), written with the symbols \(Q_\nu\) (x = q) or \(G_\nu\) (x = g), e.g. \((Q_x, Q_y, Q_z)\) or \((G_x, G_y, G_z)\) for s = 1.

Multipolar spherical harmonics. The multipolar spherical harmonics of total rank l couple the orbital part of rank l + k with the internal basis:

\[\begin{split} O_{lm}^{(s)}(k) = i^{k+s} (-1)^{l+m} \sqrt{2l+1} \sum_{\nu} \begin{pmatrix} l+k & l & s \\ m-\nu & -m & \nu \end{pmatrix} O_{l+k, m-\nu}\, e_\nu, \qquad k = -s, \dots, s, \end{split}\]

Type of the multipole. For a fixed internal type x, the type X of the multipole is determined by the parity of s + k:

  • Range: s = 1, 2, 3, l <= 3, all 47 point groups. Key: PGMultipoleType(X, l, Gamma, n, p, s, k, x) (see Multipole key).

  • The value is the coefficient of each internal basis function (polynomials in x, y, z) and the resulting expression in terms of the internal basis symbols.

4. Atomic SAMB#

The atomic SAMB (symmetry-adapted multipole basis) are the matrices of the atomic multipole operators in the orbital basis, adapted to the point group.

  1. Atomic multipoles. The spherical multipole operators \(X_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\) of X = Q, G, T, M (Q and G even, T and M odd under time reversal), with s = 0 (orbital) and s = 1 (spin-dependent), k = -s, ..., s. Their matrix elements between orbitals of ranks L1 and L2 follow from the Wigner-Eckart theorem.

    • For s = 1, the internal degree of freedom is fixed to the spin, an axial vector: the case x = g of the PG multipolar harmonics. \(X_{lm}^{(1)}(k)\) couples an orbital multipole of rank l + k with the spin. In the PG multipolar harmonics the orbital part \(O_{l+k,m}\) is always a polar function, while here the orbital multipole can be polar (Q, T) or axial (G, M, e.g. the orbital angular momentum). So the rule there for x = g is taken relative to the orbital multipole: X has the polar or axial character opposite to that of the orbital multipole if s + k is even, and the same one if s + k is odd. In addition, the spin is odd under time reversal, so X has the opposite time-reversal parity to the orbital multipole.

    • E.g. the spin magnetic dipole \(\mathbb{M}_{1}^{(1,-1;a)}(T_{1g})\) is the orbital monopole Q (rank 0, polar, even) with the spin: s + k = 0 even gives axial and the spin gives odd, i.e. M. The spin-orbit coupling \(\boldsymbol{l} \cdot \boldsymbol{s}\) is \(\sqrt{3}\, \mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(1,1;a)}(A_{1g})\) for the p orbitals: the orbital angular momentum M (rank 1, axial, odd) with the spin, s + k = 2 even gives polar and the spin gives even, i.e. Q.

  2. Symmetry adaptation. With \(U\) of the point-group harmonics of the type of the whole multipole (polar for Q, T, axial for G, M), \(X_{l\Gamma n}^{(s)}(k)[\gamma] = \sum_m U_{m\gamma} X_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\).

  3. Normalization. Each component has the Frobenius norm 1 in a diagonal block (L1 = L2), and 1/sqrt(2) in an off-diagonal block (norm 1 together with its hermitian conjugate block (L2, L1)).

  • Bases: lg (s = 0 only), lgs, and jml.

  • Key: PGMultipoleType(X, l, Gamma, n, -1, s, k, "q") (see Multipole key). Value: (dim, n1, n2), the matrix of each component for the n1 orbitals of rank L1 and the n2 orbitals of rank L2.

  • Order: Q, G, T, M, then s, k, l, Gamma, n. All 47 point groups.

Examples (Oh). For the p orbitals (lg, block (1, 1), basis px, py, pz):

  • \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(a)}(A_{1g}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\mathrm{diag}(1, 1, 1)\), the charge;

  • \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{(a)}(E_{g}) = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,\mathrm{diag}(-1, -1, 2),\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 0)\right)\), the orbital quadrupole;

  • \(\mathbb{M}_{1}^{(a)}(T_{1g})\): \((l_x, l_y, l_z)/\sqrt{2}\), the orbital angular momentum, e.g. the x component is \(l_x/\sqrt{2}\), with the matrix element \(\langle p_y | l_x | p_z \rangle / \sqrt{2} = -i/\sqrt{2}\).

For the s orbital with spin (lgs, block (0, 0), basis (s, u), (s, d)): \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(a)}(A_{1g})\) is \(\sigma_0/\sqrt{2}\), and \(\mathbb{M}_{1}^{(1,-1;a)}(T_{1g})\) is \((\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z)/\sqrt{2}\), the spin.

5. Cluster SAMB#

The cluster SAMB of a Wyckoff position are functions on its N equivalent sites (or bonds), which form bases of the irreps of the group. They are given for the 37 real point groups and the 230 space groups.

  • site. Functions on the N sites of a site Wyckoff position (e.g. 4b). They are real, have X = Q, and form a complete orthonormal set (N functions).

  • bond. Functions on the N bonds of a bond Wyckoff position. The label 2b@1o is a bond Wyckoff position 2b (2 bonds) whose centers are at the site Wyckoff position 1o. Two kinds are put together:

    • symmetric bond (the same value for the two directions of a bond, a real hopping): X = Q, real values, a complete orthonormal set (N functions);

    • anti-symmetric bond (opposite signs for the two directions, an imaginary hopping): i times the anti-symmetric function, with X = T (polar) or M (axial), pure imaginary values.

    Q and T/M are orthonormal separately; they are orthogonal to each other as hermitian bond matrices (different time-reversal parity).

  • Key: PGMultipoleType(X, l, Gamma, n, p, 0, 0, "q") (see Multipole key). (X, l, Gamma, n) names the point-group harmonics that the function corresponds to (T and M with those of Q and G), and p distinguishes functions sharing it.

  • Value: (dim, N), the values on the N sites (bonds) for each component; each component has norm 1.

  • Order: Gamma (character table), and in bond, Q before T/M in each Gamma.

Examples (D4h).

  • Site 2a: two sites at \((0, 0, \pm z)\). \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(s)}(A_{1g}) = (1, 1)/\sqrt{2}\) (uniform) and \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}^{(s)}(A_{2u}) = (1, -1)/\sqrt{2}\) (a dipole along z).

  • Bond 2b@1o: two bonds through the origin, along x and along y. \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(b)}(A_{1g}) = (1, 1)/\sqrt{2}\), \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{(b)}(B_{1g}) = (-1, 1)/\sqrt{2}\) (real hoppings), and \(\mathbb{T}_{1}^{(b)}(E_{u},a) = \left((i, 0), (0, i)\right)\) (imaginary hoppings): the x component lives on the bond along x, the y component on the bond along y.

6. Response tensors#

A response tensor of rank 0 to 4 relates two physical quantities, e.g. a current and an electric field. For each of the 157 magnetic point groups, MultiPieGen gives the tensors allowed by symmetry.

  • Type X by the parity under the spatial inversion and the time reversal: Q (polar, time-reversal even), G (axial, even), T (polar, odd), M (axial, odd).

  • opt specifies the part of the tensor by the symmetry of its indices: s symmetric and a anti-symmetric in a pair of indices, as in the table below (for a polar, time-reversal-even tensor X = Q; the other types have the same relations). The indices before ; form the pair (or triple) of the rows of the matrix, and those after it the columns. The index sets are c = [x, y, z], v = [xx, yy, zz, yz, zx, xy] (symmetric pairs), a = [yz, zx, xy] (anti-symmetric pairs), u = [xx, yy, zz, yz, zx, xy, zy, xz, yx] (all pairs), and t = [xxx, yyy, zzz, yyz, zzx, xxy, yzz, zxx, xyy, xyz] (symmetric triples). “independent” is the number of independent parameters of the part.

  • (X, l, Gamma, n) always identifies a point-group harmonics (its xyz polynomial and \(U\)); T and M use the harmonics of Q and G. n is never renumbered, so different data with the same (X, l, Gamma, n) refer to the same harmonics.

  • p appears only where several entries share (X, l, Gamma, n, s, k, x), e.g. two cluster SAMB of the same harmonics on a Wyckoff position.

  • Scalar quantities (point-group harmonics, cluster SAMB) have s = 0, k = 0, x = "q".

  • A key names a whole multiplet. Its value holds all components together, in the order of the components of the irrep (rows of the array).
